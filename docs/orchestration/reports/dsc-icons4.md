from 165 to **241** PNGs, all through the existing Blender item pipeline (`tools/render_icons.py` plus
the `tools/blender/items/*.py` builders) in the established style, byte-deterministic.

Nothing outside the icon set changed: no scenes, scripts, data tables or other art. The only non-render
edits are the six builder modules above (all under `tools/blender/items/`).

## How it was made

Each id is one builder registered in its module's `ITEMS` dict. Tiers and families are generated by
factories (one tool silhouette per material, one armour plate per slot) so a family stays visually
consistent; the tier is carried by the blade or plate material, not by a different shape.

Render: `Blender -b --factory-startup -P tools/render_icons.py -- --only <ids>` — Cycles, one CPU
thread, seed from `zlib.crc32(item_id)`, 512 px render downsampled to 256 px. Roughly 16 s per item.

Determinism: `--verify` re-renders into a temporary directory and byte-compares with the committed PNG.
A sample of ten new ids (leather_helm, steel_cuirass, copper_bracers, iron_greaves, basic_bag,
woven_bag, specialized_storage_bag, moss, steel_sword, antivenom) came back `REPRO PASS`, and every one
of the 241 committed PNGs still hashes to the `sha256` in the manifest.

Godot: every new `.png.import` needed `compress/mode=1`, `compress/lossy_quality=0.8`,
`mipmaps/generate=true` (Godot 4.7 writes mode=4 / 0.7 / false by default and `t_iconart` requires the
project settings). All 76 were patched and re-imported.

## Artistic decisions worth keeping

- **Hard edges read at 48 px; lumps do not.** The bags went through four organic passes (gourd, potato,
  cauldron) before landing on rounded-rectangle prisms with a stitched hem; that version reads
  immediately. Armour used prisms with a bulged `deform` from the start and needed far fewer attempts.
- **Attached means seated.** Free-standing straps, crests, studs and flaps read as debris or as boards
  nailed on. This cost the helm three passes (crest hoop → fin row → seated crest), the cuirass two
  (shoulder loops floating beside the plate → straps seated on the top corners) and the bags five.
- **64 KB PNG budget.** Cycles noise on procedural textures pushes a 256 px PNG over the cap and the
  renderer drops the file without writing it. `woven_bag` hit this five times (65–72 KB) and was finally
  given flat colours with the plait expressed as geometry (55 KB).
- **Bevels are not reproducible.** `bevel()` in `kit.py` documents that its evaluated mesh ordering
  varies between processes; the new geometry passes `bev=0` wherever two faces could be coplanar.
- Tier palettes: flint cream-grey, obsidian violet-black glass, bone cream on a bone haft, copper orange,
  bronze gold, iron mottled dark grey, steel bright polished. Ore/ingot pairs share the palette so an
  ingot's metal matches the tools of the same name.

## Verified

- `bash verify_job.sh icons4 1` → `VERIFY STAGE 1 PASS` (`t_iconart` PASS, no SCRIPT ERROR):
  `[t_iconart] pngs=241 missing=0 unrendered=0`, every item rendered, so the unrendered-symbol fallback
  check is now skipped.
- Determinism sample `REPRO PASS n=10`; all 241 manifest `sha256` values match the files on disk.
- Every new PNG was inspected at full tile size and at 48 px on contact sheets; the verdict for each
  batch is recorded in `.dsc/NOTES.md`.

## Known weak spots (acceptable at 48 px, worth a pass if the icon set is revisited)

- The cuirass's shoulder straps read as two square ears above the plate, and their studs sit slightly
  proud of it.
- Leather bracers and leather greaves are the plainest of the armour set: the strap studs are pale
  blobs near the edges and the greave's plate is a narrow brown slab.
- `drip_coffee`'s filter cone reads as a basket at 48 px (the mug and dark brew carry the read), and
  `crafters_meal`'s root wedges are small.
- The four spears are the thinnest tool shapes: the long haft plus a coloured point still reads, but the
  point is doing most of the work.

## What the next agent needs

- Adding an icon: write the builder in the right module, register it in `ITEMS`, then
  `tools/render_icons.py -- --only <id>` (no `--out`) writes the PNG and the manifest entry, then a
  Godot `--import` and the `.import` sed above (otherwise `t_iconart` fails on import settings).
- Reviewing art: `contact_sheet.py -- out.png a.png b.png ...`; pass paths as one argument each.
- Any new item art must keep the `sha256` in the manifest or the determinism sample will fail.
- Reminder from the repo rules: `tools/smoke.sh` and the related UI tests are the orchestrator's gate;
  this task only ran the icon gate.
