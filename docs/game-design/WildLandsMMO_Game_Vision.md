# WildLands MMO

> **Working title:** WildLandsMMO  
> **Document type:** Game vision encyclopedia / living game wiki  
> **Status:** Intended complete game experience, not a list of features already live  
> **Genre:** Isometric 3D prehistoric survival RPG, creature taming and breeding, tribe management, manufacturing, settlement building, persistent economy, PvE and optional PvPvE  
> **Creative references:** *Durango: Wild Lands* (exploration, gathering, production), *ARK: Survival Evolved* (prehistoric creatures and breeding), *Last Day on Earth* (survival interactions), *Minecraft Skyblock* (protected-home growth and economy)

WildLands MMO is a world in which a lone human survivor can become the founder of a living prehistoric civilization. Dinosaurs are predators, companions, mounts, livestock, workers and war machines. People are distinct individuals with inherited potential, learned talents, equipment and lifetimes of experience. Resources have levels, defects and rare virtues. Buildings can become manufactured works of craftsmanship. A secure private home anchors long-term progress while dangerous islands offer discovery, valuable goods and optional conflict.

The game's identity lies in the connections among survival, people, creatures, materials, manufacturing, logistics and trade. Wealth is not just a number. It is a skilled artisan, a superior dinosaur lineage, a carefully made weapon, a safe house, a stockpile of quality resources, or a reliable supply route.

**The central fantasy:** Start with nothing. Learn to survive. Tame the wilderness. Master a craft. Raise a tribe. Build an industry. Connect with other civilizations. Venture beyond safety, and decide what your legacy will become.

## 1. The player's journey

### From survivor to civilization

WildLands MMO has two intertwined perspectives on progression. The player directly controls a specific human who walks, explores, gathers, fights, rides and makes choices. At the same time, the player's long-term achievements belong to a growing tribe with people, land, stored property, animal lineages and infrastructure.

These dimensions complement rather than replace one another. A veteran may become a highly skilled worker when the player takes control of a younger successor. The tribe retains its achievements, but the successor must develop their own skills, level and equipment eligibility. Even a powerful civilization is built out of individual people doing meaningful things.

### A natural progression arc

| Stage | What life feels like | What expands the player's possibilities |
|---|---|---|
| First survival | A dangerous wilderness, primitive tools, basic food and shelter | Gathering, crafting, first combat encounters, a safe home |
| First expeditions | Preparation matters; not every island can be safely exploited | Hunting, specialized tools, cargo and temporary camps |
| Animal companionship | Dinosaurs change how the survivor travels and fights | Taming, feeding, bonding, riding and animal training |
| Skilled production | Resources become components with meaningful origins and properties | Processing, cooking, better workshops and specialized equipment |
| Living settlement | Human inhabitants make a community rather than a collection of menus | Homes, workstations, workers, family, education and logistics |
| Regional industry | Quality goods and transport connect multiple islands and professions | Material specialization, larger builds, harbors and trade |
| High-end expeditions | Advanced animals, materials and equipment open difficult territory | Personal leveling, elite creatures, specialized expedition parties |
| Optional frontier | Prepared settlements face costly competition and siege | Defensible outposts, siege logistics, risky cargo and captivity |

The journey is not a rigid campaign. Players may emphasize exploration, breeding, crafting, building, trade or combat while still participating in the wider world.

### The essential recurring cycle

A survivor prepares at home, travels with a deliberate goal, encounters environmental challenges, finds resources or opportunities, makes choices about risk and carrying capacity, returns or extracts valuables, and uses the results to improve people, animals, equipment or settlement capabilities. Those improvements enable more ambitious expeditions and better economic specialization.

Every excursion can advance both the personal character and the permanent civilization. Every valuable item has a story of how it was found, produced or acquired.

## 2. The world and its islands

Islands differ in climate, terrain, ecology, material level, available creatures, accessibility, instability and exposure to conflict. A prehistoric grassland should feel different from a dense forest, wetland, volcanic region or high-level hostile wilderness.

Wildlife inhabits places according to species behavior. Small pack predators create a different threat from a roaming apex predator or an enormous armored herbivore. Creatures can hunt, flee, defend territory or respond to groups as their species suggests.

### Island categories

| Location | Role in the game | Relationship to danger |
|---|---|---|
| **Private home island** | Permanent, safe family and economic base | Never raidable or attacked by enemy creatures |
| **Unstable expedition island** | Temporary source of exploration, hunting, gathering and field camps | Periodic resets threaten remaining installations and supplies |
| **PvE expedition settlement** | Persistent or temporary remote operation focused on wilderness activity | May face raids from animals and dinosaurs, not player raids |
| **PvPvE frontier settlement** | Optional high-end outpost and strategic holding | Hostile wildlife and player-directed settlement raids |

The island itself is not the target of a player raid. In the frontier, the vulnerable assets are **placed settlements, infrastructure, personnel and exposed cargo**, not the permanent terrain.

### Unstable islands

Unstable islands reset their resources and world state. A temporary base has value only for as long as it supports an expedition or can be successfully evacuated. Some structures are intentionally cheap and disposable; others are valuable enough to package and extract.

When an unstable island resets, exposed construction is affected by the island lifecycle. The precise recovery percentage remains unresolved: the design has contemplated automatically salvaging some invested construction materials to a special vault, while another concept describes abandoning anything not evacuated. It is not the same transaction as upgrading a safe home, where the building-material return is explicitly complete.

The most powerful expedition leaders succeed not only by finding rare goods but by deciding what to risk, what to build, what to move, and when to leave.

## 3. Private homes and permanent progression

Every player owns a private home island. It is the permanent sanctuary of their civilization, where residents, animals, workshops, storage and buildings are safe from attacks or raids. Players do not lose their whole life's work because another tribe chooses to attack their personal island.

### Purchasing a new home

Home-island advancement occurs through a **paid relocation**, rather than indefinitely expanding the current terrain. The player chooses and previews a larger or higher-tier home, confirms the purchase, and moves to a fresh island. The abandoned home is replaced, not carried forward as an additional owned private island.

Old buildings are automatically dismantled after the confirmed move. **100% of the actual construction materials invested in those buildings** are returned through a dedicated salvage vault, distinct from ordinary storage. Residents, tame animals and stored goods move by separate transfer rules.

A larger private home provides more room and improved natural resources. At the upper end, private-home resource nodes reach approximately level **50 to 55**. Materials at level **60 and beyond** require ventures elsewhere, so a peaceful, protected economic base still depends on expeditions.

### A secure foundation, not free prosperity

Safety protects accumulated progress, but high-quality goods still require effort. Workshops consume supplies; animals need care; food ages; people must train; expeditions need resources. A protected home enables persistence without removing meaningful survival and economic choices.

## 4. Survivors and personal progression

Humans are individual characters, not interchangeable units of workforce. Each has a name, identity, appearance, life stage, personal level, acquired experience, equipment, inherited potential and learned proficiencies.

### Levels and equipment

Character level is earned personally. A level-1 successor is not entitled to fight or equip as their level-60 predecessor simply because they share a tribe.

Equipment has a **strict personal level requirement**. A human cannot equip an item above their own eligible level merely because it was inherited, bought, gifted, looted or crafted by someone else. This preserves a meaningful distinction between buying expensive gear and developing a capable individual.

The tribe permanently remembers its high-end island unlock when any member reaches level 60. But a personally controlled character must themselves be level **60** to enter the optional level-60 PvPvE frontier. Switching back to a qualified veteran is a valid way to use the established tribe's access.

### Innate potential and learned competence

**Dexterity** represents inherited potential for control, accuracy, quick handling and detail-oriented work. It matters in weapon handling and trades such as tailoring.

**Might** represents inherited potential for forceful movement, physical attacks, mining, heavy smithing and other strength-dependent tasks.

**Endurance** supports sustained work, construction, effort and recovery-related performance.

**Intelligence** contributes to research and leadership, including the coordination of squads or packs. A capable leader may combine tactical understanding with physical credibility.

Inherited potential does not substitute for experience. A naturally gifted novice must still develop relevant skills. Personal career progression, trained characteristics, and innate values remain separate parts of a character's identity.

## 5. Tribes, family and generations

A tribe is a population of persistent human individuals sharing a civilization. Members can be controlled directly, trained, assigned work, equipped, educated and entrusted with expeditions. Former player avatars remain part of the community rather than disappearing when the player changes characters.

### Workers as people

The master blacksmith may be a veteran who once led expeditions. A hunter may know a region and its creatures. A gifted researcher may contribute through knowledge instead of combat. An inexperienced recruit can carry supplies but may not yet be capable of advanced construction.

Human abilities have opportunity costs. A tribesperson operating a turret is not also mining ore elsewhere. The worker's location, tools, fatigue, skill and actual access to materials matter.

### Family and children

Human reproduction is a significant, resource-intensive long-term investment, even for prosperous tribes. Children inherit innate potential, but do not inherit their parents' earned levels, experience or learned skills.

Growing children require time, nourishment and care. Food quality during development can influence their expressed development. Education and mentorship create opportunities to cultivate natural potential into practical skill.

Generations carry identity and history. A famous warrior's descendant may possess promising innate talent yet still be untested; an elderly artisan may be less suited to expeditions but invaluable as a mentor.

### Continuity after loss

Individual humans may be lost through dangerous frontier activity. If a tribe loses every human, a new baseline survivor gives the player a way to recover. The protected home remains a foundation even after severe personal setbacks.

## 6. Dinosaurs, taming and genetics

Dinosaurs are central to life in WildLands MMO. They are creatures with anatomy, behavior, needs and individual potential, not merely alternate equipment slots.

### Wild ecology

Species occupy suitable regions and behave according to their roles. A solitary territorial apex predator creates a different encounter from coordinated raptors. Enormous herbivores can be threatening through physical mass and reach even if they are not aggressive hunters.

### Capture and bonds

Taming is species-dependent. Preparation, combat control, food, timing, special conditions and animal handling may be important to a successful capture. A captured dinosaur becomes a persistent individual with its own attributes, experience, feeding needs, equipment, training and future value.

### Genes and breeding

Dinosaurs possess inherited individual values, while training and personal levels contribute developed performance. A rare wild specimen with exceptional innate traits can be worth preserving for a lineage. Selective breeding is a long-term source of distinctive animals, not simply a mechanism for printing combat power.

Lineages may emphasize endurance, hauling, combat capability, mobility, resilience or another species-appropriate aptitude. Even animals of the same species can become meaningfully different investments.

### Mount roles

The intended battlefield vocabulary includes seven role families: **Cavalry/Courser**, **Skirmisher/Flanker**, **Charger/Breaker**, **Bulwark/Tank**, **Berserker/Ravager**, **Tyrant/Apex Dominator**, and **Titan/Siege Beast**.

These roles describe battlefield behavior and practical function, not universal damage types. Dinosaurs may use different forms of attack with different strengths and reach.

A Tyrannosaurus rex is intended to be tameable and rideable in the mature game, with its own high-end capture and balance challenges. Brontosaurus is intended as a distinct giant herbivore, not just an unexamined rename of another sauropod.

## 7. Expeditions, parties and mounted travel

Travel requires preparation. An expedition may carry food, medicine, spare tools, weapons, saddles, traps, building kits and empty capacity for valuable finds.

The companion system offers **three shared active expedition slots**. A deployed human rider and their dinosaur can function as one composite companion slot. That does not create an extra human or let a member be in two places at once.

### Why composition matters

A light scout may move quickly and reveal opportunities. A sturdy defender can protect a gathering team. A cargo animal makes a resource trip profitable. An expert handler may improve an animal's coordinated behavior. A siege beast provides enormous force at equally enormous logistical cost.

### Physical riding

Mounted humans actually sit on their dinosaurs. Weapon reach depends on saddle height, weapon length, swing path and target position. A sword held by a rider high on a giant Tyrannosaurus cannot strike a small creature far below the rider's feet merely because the target lies inside a generic radius.

The dinosaur's bite, stomp or tail attack remains distinct from the rider's weapon attack. Mounted teamwork emphasizes coordination rather than automatically granting two full, independent attack schedules.

## 8. Resources, material levels and traits

Gathering is both a survival activity and a search for valuable material qualities. Logs, bones, stone, fiber, ore, scutes, teeth, horns, hides and sinew may vary by level, source and properties.

### Material character

Resources are not almost always bland and interchangeable. Most lots carry consequential **negative traits**; unusually desirable positive traits are rare. A poor-quality lot may be brittle, heavy, unstable, inefficient or otherwise flawed. A rare lot may be resilient, shock-absorbing or especially suitable for a trade.

High-level material is often necessary for advanced products, but level and actual material character both matter. Two materials of the same nominal type may have very different values in the market.

### Two-material composites

A central processing concept joins **two eligible material lots into one composite**. The processed material carries the signed properties of both sources, beneficial and detrimental.

For example, a resilient bone piece and a shock-absorbing piece can yield a reinforced component with both advantages. A brittle input may pass that weakness into the finished component even if the other input is extraordinary.

When an identical trait is inherited from multiple sources, its total benefit is bounded by diminishing returns. Repeated processing cannot infinitely duplicate a single rare property. The origin of useful qualities matters.

The precise chance of trait rolls, numerical scaling, merging curve and trait catalog are still part of the balancing vision, rather than settled universal values.

### Material properties beyond weapons

Trait-bearing materials can affect tool longevity, armor, structures, food, saddles, boats and workshop operations when their properties are relevant to the finished object. A building derives characteristics from **materials actually delivered to its construction site**, not from a blueprint with imaginary material bonuses.

## 9. Processing, crafting and the manufacturing economy

Civilization is built through increasingly sophisticated material transformation. The progression from crude survival tools to specialized goods is also a progression in knowledge, institutions and trade relationships.

### Primitive beginnings

Early tools and weapons rely on readily gathered stone, wood, bone, fiber and hides. They help the player gather more efficiently, defend themselves and handle basic survival tasks.

### Intermediate products

Advanced equipment requires processed ingredients. A production chain might transform:

- **Wood:** logs to split wood, planks, treated stock and reinforced hafts.
- **Bone:** raw dense bone to carved ribs, reinforced bone blocks and shaped components.
- **Sinew:** tendon to cord to braided cable.
- **Animal armor:** scutes to worked protective plates.
- **Metal:** mined ore to smelted ingot, refined bar and shaped forged fittings.

Processing depth creates room for specialists. A skilled woodworker, tanner, boneworker and smith each create components useful to someone else's final assembly.

### Three equipment eras

The six defining weapon classes appear across **three eras**: early stone/bone, middle-era animal composites and early copper, and late refined iron. This yields **18 defining weapon builds**, each rooted in real gathered and processed resources.

Metalworking represents a major technological breakthrough. Raw ore is not magically equivalent to a finished steel-like component at an ordinary workbench. Even in the later metal era, dinosaur-derived materials remain relevant.

### Worksites and professions

Crafting and building belong to a connected economic experience. Blueprints are discovered, unlocked or crafted through a shared crafting world; workshops and physical construction sites turn material inputs and labor into finished goods. A craftsman makes a valuable product because the work, resources, tools and experience behind it matter.

## 10. Food, preservation and recovery

Food is a living resource with time, temperature and preservation considerations. Perishables decay whether stored in a bag, container, expedition camp or protected home, and continue aging while the player is offline.

**Freshness is different from item durability.** At zero freshness, a food item becomes an inedible rotten material that may be useful for compost, bait or another secondary purpose rather than simply disappearing.

Cooking, smoking, drying, salting and pickling extend food usefulness through different processes. Specialized storage changes preservation rate but does not reset food age when goods are transferred. Advanced airtight storage might exist if it fits the game's eventual technological ceiling.

### Traits and cooking

Ingredient traits influence meals as temporary, visible effects rather than permanent inherited genetics. An ingredient might contribute energy, a longer meal buff, useful warmth or cooling, fortification, or spoilage resistance. Other ingredients may create contamination or undesirable effects. Both positive and negative input properties matter to the cooked output.

Food quality matters to player expeditions, workers, dinosaur feed and the expensive development of children.

### Fatigue, sitting and sleep

Characters expend energy while fighting, working and traveling. Sitting in a suitable place gradually restores energy and reduces exhaustion. Sleeping offers stronger continuous recovery and can benefit from good shelter and accommodations.

A bedroom is valuable not just because it looks pleasant but because it contributes to the settlement's daily life.

## 11. Construction, rooms and packable structures

Building is centered on **blueprints, unfinished worksites and modular prefabs**, not placing every foundation, wall and roof tile independently.

### Constructing a building

A player selects a building or room blueprint, establishes a valid site on the grid, supplies required processed materials, and completes it through real labor. Some advanced construction requires a qualified builder to initiate the work even when less experienced helpers can contribute resources.

### Modular houses

Connected **2×2, 3×3 and 4×4 room modules** support recognizable architecture. Door connections, walls, roofs, floors, pillars, materials and furnishings determine how the building looks and functions.

Rooms have their own functional ambience. A proper kitchen may support better cooking. A comfortable bedroom supports rest. A workshop is a place for specialized production. Material quality contributes to condition, protection, insulation and other compatible properties.

### A building can be an economic good

A finished structure is a **unique manufactured item**. It can carry maker identity, component history, material properties, appearance, condition and ownership. Under appropriate conditions, it can be packed, transported, sold and redeployed.

A builder can specialize in selling completed buildings, not merely supplying construction ingredients.

Packing is different from dismantling. Packing preserves the structure's identity; dismantling recovers its invested materials. A packed structure and its placed counterpart must never exist as two independent copies of the same manufactured work.

Occupied structures, nonempty storage and loaded defensive equipment introduce real constraints on safe packaging.

## 12. Workers, jobs and automation

A mature settlement functions as a production network populated by actual humans.

Workers can gather, mine, fell trees, farm, craft, haul, build, research, guard, handle animals and operate equipment. Jobs depend on the worker's aptitude, skill, energy, gear and access to physical resources. Materials have to reach a worksite before production can occur.

A **Monitoring Stand** gives the settlement a recognizable place for directing worker preferences and job priorities. Workers may favor their specializations but remain bound by their actual capabilities.

### Tools and resource conservation

Work tools wear down. Automated workers normally stop using a work tool at **one point of durability remaining** rather than breaking valuable equipment through unsupervised repetition.

Hauling, ingredient supply, storage capacity, worker availability and worksite access affect throughput. Efficient automation improves organization; it does not create free goods, teleport human labor or erase the cost of supplies.

### The meaning of a productive settlement

A successful operation might involve gatherers supplying raw resources, processors making intermediate materials, artisans assembling valuable items, farmers sustaining the population, and a transport crew carrying surplus goods to a market.

That entire chain is part of one living settlement, not a disconnected set of progress bars.

## 13. Combat and weapon identities

Combat draws from two physical disciplines and three damage verbs.

| | **Impact (I)** | **Pierce (P)** | **Slash (S)** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Dexterity (D)** | **DI**: controlled throws, slingshots, aimed impact | **DP**: bows, blowdarts and precise thrusting or firing | **DS**: whips, throwing axes and controlled cutting |
| **Might (M)** | **MI**: heavy hammer, fist, stomp and crushing blows | **MP**: forceful spears, javelins, horns and spikes | **MS**: swords, axes and powerful cleaving attacks |

**Dexterity** emphasizes controlled handling; **Might** emphasizes physical force. Both may apply to melee or ranged equipment. **Impact** disrupts, **Pierce** penetrates and **Slash** cuts or rends. Delivery method, attack speed, range and AoE remain separate properties.

### Tactical weapon choice

The weapon system encourages choosing the right attack for the enemy and situation rather than equipping one universally superior damage number.

A large unarmored body may be particularly vulnerable to sustained Slash. An agile exposed target may be easier to threaten with accurate Pierce. An armored target may demand Impact force or careful targeting of exposed anatomy.

Armor tiers overlap tactically: Slash is favorable against exposed/light protection, Pierce against light/medium, and Impact against medium/heavy. These are broad intended matchups, not invincible or universally dominant rules.

### Special attack rewards

Mounted thrusts can reward real closing momentum. A well-aimed distant slingshot impact can earn a **Longshot** bonus. Berserker-style melee Slash sequences can develop **Frenzy**, rewarding consecutive successful hits rather than simple button repetition. Each bonus depends on its physical and tactical context.

Actual values, caps and timing remain balance questions.

## 14. Armor, mass, anatomy and grappling

Physical form is a gameplay rule. Creature size, body weight, equipment load and local protective coverage affect movement and combat.

### Armor is located on the body

An animal's protected back, skull or flanks should not automatically make every exposed limb invulnerable. A weapon connects with a specific region of the model. A rider's short blade must actually reach its target. A projectile must follow a plausible path.

### Mass is both force and resistance

A heavy creature can deliver devastating properly landed stomps, tackles, slams and tramples. Extra fitted armor, saddles and cargo contribute effective carried mass, but their weight also burdens acceleration, turning, stamina and load comfort.

A heavy target is harder to stagger and may suffer less knockback or damage from a relative-mass attack. Stability and armor matter, but piling plates onto a tiny animal should not make it casually immune to an enormous sauropod.

### Raptor pounce and pin

Certain predatory animals can leap onto vulnerable targets, establish a pin and follow with mauling attacks. A successful pin depends on actual contact and the relative physical conditions of the two creatures.

An ordinary raptor pounce into intact heavy armor should **recoil or fail to secure the pin**, rather than magically passing through protection and triggering the entire grapple sequence. Heavy armor does not make the target immune to every other form of crushing or grappling.

### A giant herbivore as a boss

A Brontosaurus-type titan may combine extraordinarily high health with exposed-body Slash vulnerability, an exceptionally long damaging tail whip and a deadly close-range stomp. Its threat is defined by anatomy, reach, timing and mass, not merely a huge health bar.

## 15. Siege weapons and turrets

Defensive emplacements and siege weapons are physical manufactured goods. They occupy positions, possess operating limits and consume ammunition.

### Crewed weapons

Some weapons require an actual human operator. The operator has to be at the emplacement, able to act, and appropriately equipped or trained. Their presence has an opportunity cost to the tribe.

### Automatic weapons

Other weapons operate automatically through plausible mechanical systems. Automatic turrets still require loaded ammunition, have limited arcs, ranges and triggering logic, and may need workers to resupply them.

### Siege logistics

A powerful gun without ammunition is simply an expensive object. Storing, transporting, loading and prioritizing ammunition become important parts of conflict. Different weapons may require different rounds and have different reload, penetration and targeting characteristics.

Stationary weapons and their mobile equivalents share this physical economy without becoming identical platforms.

## 16. Dinosaur platform saddles

The largest eligible dinosaurs can carry **platform saddles**, converting a living mount into a mobile defensive or siege structure.

### Equipment limits

| Platform component | Maximum installed |
|---|---:|
| Crewed siege weapon | **1** |
| Automatic turrets | **2** |
| Shared finite ammunition box | **1** |

The ammunition box serves the installed weapons and contains real, limited ammunition. Saddle, equipment, loaded ammo, crew, armor and cargo contribute to the dinosaur's carried weight.

### Two valid crew arrangements

1. **Player drives, tribesperson guns.** The player steers the dinosaur while a real tribe member operates its crewed siege weapon.
2. **Tribesperson drives, player guns.** A trained tribe member steers the dinosaur while the player personally aims and fires the crewed weapon.

Automatic turrets do not each require another dedicated gunner, but they do require ammunition and operate within their mechanical limits.

No configuration grants phantom workers or free extra people. A driver is committed to driving; a gunner is committed to the weapon. A large platform saddle should feel powerful, heavy and logistically demanding.

### A moving fortress with physical weaknesses

A siege dinosaur can support an expedition, advance on fortified positions, cover workers or repel attackers. Its strength depends on terrain, carrying comfort, acceleration, visibility, crew survival, ammunition supply, weapon arcs and a suitable species' anatomy. It is not a limitless mobile gun tower.

## 17. Boats, harbors and resource transport

A successful expedition must return its gains to safety. Boats and harbors turn travel into a logistical undertaking.

Cargo capacity depends on both the **boat's level and traits** and the **harbor's level and traits**. A high-quality boat cannot by itself erase a badly equipped loading operation.

During a remote raid, a boat can still depart for the private home. **Once genuinely underway toward home, the vessel, crew and cargo cannot be intercepted or raided.** The risk lies in the expedition, loading area, contested stores and departure preparations.

This creates dramatic decisions about which goods, people and animals to load first and when to leave. Cargo has to occupy real capacity; escaping with one shipment does not also duplicate those same supplies in a remote warehouse.

## 18. Trading, specializations and the MMO economy

WildLands MMO's multiplayer ambition is a world where player specializations become valuable to one another.

An explorer may source rare ingredients. A breeder may produce superior riding lines. A smith may manufacture tailored weapons. A builder may sell ready-to-place houses. A food specialist may provision expeditions. A logistician may reliably move valuable cargo across dangerous territory.

### What gives an item value?

Value comes from scarcity, provenance, useful properties, level, quality, craftsmanship, training and demand. A particularly resilient composite or a well-bred dinosaur represents more than its catalog ID.

The game's economy is supported by persistent ownership and meaningful production costs. Trading high-level goods can make a player wealthy or well prepared, but a lower-level character still cannot bypass personal equipment requirements.

### PvE and trade remain real playstyles

A player should be able to pursue prosperity through non-PvP economic activity. The optional frontier introduces more risk and rare opportunities but should not invalidate peaceful specializations or turn raiding into the only rational way to progress.

How players physically meet, communicate, list goods, form guild-like relationships or share open-world spaces remains a subject for the multiplayer vision workshop.

## 19. The PvE and PvPvE frontier

WildLands MMO distinguishes secure ownership from chosen exposure.

**Private home:** Permanently safe from raids and enemy attacks. Its importance grows with the player's investment.

**Remote PvE outpost:** Exposed to environmental threats such as dinosaurs, not player settlement raids.

**Remote PvPvE outpost:** Exposed to wildlife and eligible attacks from other players. The terrain itself is not raided, only the settlement and its vulnerable assets.

### Frontier access and fairness

The tribe can retain its endgame geographic unlock permanently, but the personally controlled human needs their own qualifying level to enter the level-60 frontier.

Raiding is intended to be expensive, difficult and realistically defensible. A dominant regional group should feel powerful but should not be able to cheaply overwhelm beginners without meaningful constraints.

There are **no scheduled player-selected vulnerability windows**. Defense and fairness instead depend on a combination of settlement strength, raid cost, regional limits, defenses and other still-to-be-finalized safeguards.

A raid might threaten exposed supplies, equipped gear, dinosaurs or humans, but it can never reach into the protected private home to erase long-term civilization progress.

## 20. Captives and ransom

Characters can become captives through eligible frontier conflicts. Captivity treats a human or animal as a persistent individual, not a disposable generic loot token.

### The 72-hour decision

The original owner has **72 real-world hours** to redeem a captive at a **game-calculated ransom price**.

**If paid in time:** The original owner receives the same individual with inherited potential, previous personal level, learned skills and trained characteristics preserved.

**If the ransom expires unpaid:** The captor may acquire the same individual. Their original inherited traits, identity, pedigree and adult appearance remain, but they begin a **new level-one adult career**, with earned experience, learned skills and trained advancement reset. They do not become a child again.

Ransom pricing, exchange, notification, allegiance, claiming and server-authoritative custody still require final design decisions.

The system creates an unusual distinction between a person's valuable innate lineage and their lifetime of acquired skill.

## 21. Interface and visual identity

The game is viewed through an isometric 3D world, with the survival and settlement experience remaining tactile and legible.

### Interaction language

Relevant actions appear contextually around resources, creatures, buildings and equipment. A consistent family of hexagonal controls helps the player recognize movement, interaction, inventory, crafting, animal management and combat actions.

Mouse and keyboard are the main desktop interaction target; touch input remains an important supported experience. Dense information should not require obscure menus to understand what is happening.

### Useful information, not hidden spreadsheets

Items show relevant level, durability, material traits, condition and effects. Status effects reveal numerical values and durations on hover or tap. Inventories support batch selection, locking, dropping, destruction and organization. Players can understand why equipment is effective or ineffective.

### Visual identity

WildLands MMO uses deliberate, readable stylized low-poly 3D presentation. Dinosaurs should have recognizable silhouettes, size-dependent movement and animations that communicate weight and temperament. Architecture should reflect material choices and local environment. Distinct regions should feel rich and inhabited.

The art direction aims for the readability and contextual richness of classic prehistoric survival RPGs while developing its own assets, creatures, architecture and identity.

## 22. Stories that emerge from the systems

### The rare-material craftsman

A hunter finds an unusually resilient bone among otherwise flawed spoils. Another expedition brings home a compatible shock-absorbing material. At a specialized workshop, a craftsman creates a composite that retains both valuable characteristics. An advanced weaponmaker turns it into a sought-after component. The resulting weapon has a particular history and value because its ingredients and craftsmanship are genuinely exceptional.

### The first industrial home

A once-solitary survivor has a house, storage and a workshop. A second human specializes in woodworking. A veteran survivor becomes a builder. A farmer keeps the group fed. An animal hauls materials. These people and resources support increasingly ambitious construction until the home becomes a coordinated production village.

### The breeder's legacy

Two impressive but imperfect dinosaurs produce an offspring with promising inherited qualities. The breeder raises and trains it, compares its abilities to others in the lineage, and eventually chooses whether to keep it as breeding stock, use it for expeditions or trade it to another player.

### The unstable-island expedition

An expedition lands with tools, portable infrastructure, mounts and food. Workers gather valuable resources while guards protect the operation. As the reset approaches, the expedition leader must decide which unfinished projects to abandon, which structures to package, and how much cargo to extract before opportunities disappear.

### The moving siege beast

A large dinosaur carries a platform saddle, one crewed weapon, two mechanical turrets and a shared ammunition box. A real tribesperson drives while the player aims the main weapon. Advancing makes the platform dangerous, but heavy equipment burdens mobility and the crew cannot continue indefinitely without ammunition.

### The difficult frontier victory

A well-prepared tribe ventures into PvPvE territory to secure a valuable remote settlement. Another group attacks. Equipment and defenses matter, but so do transport, ammunition, decisions about extraction and the skill of the human operators. Some exposed valuables may be lost, but the private home and long-developed civilization remain secure.

## 23. The defining promises

**Progress has a home.** A player's private island, family wealth and long-term efforts are protected from other players' raids.

**People are individual.** Careers, inherited potential, learning, aging and generations matter.

**Dinosaurs are living partners.** Their ecology, anatomy, genetics, training, mass and physical roles shape the world.

**Resources have character.** Imperfect lots and rare exceptional traits make exploration and manufacturing interesting.

**Craftsmanship creates wealth.** Processed materials, player specialization and unique manufactured structures have economic value.

**The world has physical constraints.** A person occupies one role and location. Ammunition is finite. Cargo has weight and capacity. A weapon must reach its target.

**Civilizations are systems.** Workers, farms, workshops, animals, stores and transport routes interact.

**Danger is chosen and prepared for.** PvE and protected economic growth are legitimate long-term paths; frontier conflict is a risky branch rather than the entire purpose of the game.

**Stories last.** An exceptional sword, dinosaur lineage, house or veteran character can retain identity and meaning across generations and trades.

## 24. Vision boundaries and open questions

The encyclopedia is a vision of the *finished* WildLands MMO world. Some systems exist in the current game in simpler form; others are intended future features. The following questions shape the finished experience and are not yet settled by this wiki:

- **Multiplayer identity:** Which parts of the world are shared and simultaneous? Do players inhabit persistent public islands, enter instances, or visit one another through hubs and markets?
- **First-hour hook:** Is the immediate fantasy survival danger, first dinosaur bond, satisfying gathering/crafting, or the beginning of a safe home?
- **Primary long-term rhythm:** How much time should players typically spend exploring, managing workers, crafting, trading and fighting?
- **Island-reset salvage:** What fraction of materials or finished buildings left on unstable islands is recoverable at reset?
- **Technological ceiling:** How primitive does the setting remain, and whether firearms, sealed preservation or other advanced devices belong in the world?
- **Social structure:** Do tribes mean a player's personal human population, an organization of many players, or both under different names?
- **Economic geography:** Where do other players physically interact and trade, and how much trade is asynchronous or through markets?
- **Mortality and custody:** Which losses are permanent outside the home, and which have recovery routes?
- **Session cadence:** How do real-time jobs, food spoilage, breeding, world resets and voyages remain compelling without requiring constant attention?
- **Commercial identity:** WildLands MMO / WildLandsMMO is a working title; a distinctive final name and brand presentation have not yet been selected.

A useful vision is specific about the experience while leaving room to choose the best exact balance and presentation. What matters most is the coherent relationship between a single vulnerable survivor and a civilization built through knowledge, creatures, people, production, travel and risk.

**WildLands MMO: From lone survivor to living prehistoric civilization.**
