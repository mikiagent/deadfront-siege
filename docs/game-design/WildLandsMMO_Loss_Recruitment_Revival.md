# WildLands MMO | Loss, Recovery, Recruitment, Captivity and Late-Game Revival

> **Status:** OWNER-CONFIRMED EXPERIENCE + WORKSHOP PROPOSALS (numerical balance and exact procedures undecided).  
> **Owner's latest direction:** Taming begins through open-world discovery, not a forced guided grant. PvE deaths can lose companions or tribe members; dropped loot is recoverable. Basic dinosaurs and people have many replacement paths. Mediocre/basic human recruits occasionally arrive by boat at safe private homes; more and better candidates arrive at frontier/PvPvE outposts. Tribe workers autonomously mine/log, with variable pace and item quality set partly by site level. Solo play is fully viable; late-game multiplayer, co-op, raids, outposts and PvPvE are additional choices. Killed human survivors/tribe members should have an **extremely costly, seldom-used late-game revival route**.  
> **Existing locked captive rule:** Prisoners have a **72-real-hour game-priced ransom window**. Paid ransom returns the same individual with their original IV, personal levels, learned skills and EVs. Unpaid captives claimed by attackers become level-1 adults retaining identity, appearance, pedigree and inherited IVs but resetting earned progression. Being **captured** is different from being **dead**.

## Why loss matters without becoming a dead end

Players grow attached to exceptional individuals, carefully selected dinosaur lineages and rare composite gear. Taking one of these into danger should be a deliberate choice. At the same time, WildLands MMO is a long-lived sandbox, not a game where one mistake destroys a civilization.

**The core risk philosophy:** Risk what you can afford to lose. Protect what you cannot replace easily. A protected home preserves your civilization even when an expedition goes terribly wrong.

A player can recover by raising ordinary companions, employing new arrivals, breeding, gathering, mining, logging and rebuilding. Basic resources should not depend on defeating rival players or spending money in a cash shop. Rare excellence, not basic competence, is what makes an expedition's stakes emotionally and economically significant.

## First dinosaur: earned through discovery

The player's first dinosaur is discovered through exploration, observation and an authentic taming opportunity in the wild. There is **no mandatory guided grant or guaranteed starter pet**. Early encounters may communicate the possibility of future companionship, even if a player chooses not to tame one immediately. This respects the balanced first 20–30 minute survival/crafting/dinosaur introduction while keeping the world open-ended.

Basic tamable species should remain obtainable through multiple routes and regions, not a single irreplaceable early encounter.

## Recruitment and replacement economy

### Occasional home-island arrivals

A small boat occasionally visits or arrives at the protected private home with a potential new **human tribe recruit**. Home arrivals are **generally low-stat or mediocre**, occasionally useful and rarely exceptional. They create a dependable recovery avenue for solitary or unlucky players. A recruit is a distinct person with age, innate potential, needs, learned abilities and possible specialization, not a free labor counter.

### Frontier and outpost recruitment

Remote frontier and PvPvE settlements attract **more frequent arrivals and higher probabilities of superior inherited potential**, though they require exposure, infrastructure and protection. High-value candidates introduce genuine risk: a skilled recruit at a contested outpost can be lost or captured before reaching home.

A peaceful player can still nurture lineages, train ordinary individuals and obtain competent people without PvP. PvPvE offers an accelerated *opportunity and exposure*, not the exclusive legal source of worthwhile humans.

### Candidate recruiting formula (not approved)

`Expected_visits_per_day = Base_visits × Location_attraction × Housing_capacity_factor × Safety_and_supply_factor`.

One **hypothetical** comparison:

| Location | Illustrative candidate arrivals | Illustrative excellent innate-potential chance | Risk |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Small private home | 1 visiting recruit every 3–5 real days | 0.5% | Safe to receive |
| Developed private home | 1 every 1–2 days | 1% | Safe to receive |
| Well-equipped PvE outpost | 1–2 per day | 2–3% | Wildlife |
| Active PvPvE frontier outpost | 3–5 per day | 5–8% | Player raids/captivity |

**These are examples only.** Whether arrivals are real-time, in-game time, random or scheduled; whether free to hire, costly to host, limited by population, or influenced by reputation is open. The wide gap is intentional for discussion: does it *tempt* PvPvE participation, or practically force it?

### Recruitment rarity and growth

A new arrival's current skills and **inherited potential** should be evaluated separately. A mediocre adult can become a valuable trained worker. An exceptional untrained adult may be valuable for rare lineage development even before they learn a profession.

Housing, food, healthcare, education and equipment limit how many people a settlement can effectively support. More arrivals do not automatically mean exponentially free production.

## Distinguishing four kinds of setback

| Event | Outcome | Recovery path |
|---|---|---|
| Dropped expedition loot | Gear/supplies remain at risk, **recoverable** subject to world danger and any recovery limit | Return to the site, recover the load, possibly lose it if circumstances change |
| Dead human survivor or tribe member | That individual's active career ends unless a **late-game revival** succeeds | Ordinary replacement, descendant, or extraordinarily costly revival |
| Dead dinosaur | A valued animal can be permanently lost | Capture, breed or train another; **dinosaur revival not currently requested/approved** |
| Captured living human or dinosaur | Separate custodial status; owner may pay or exchange within 72 real hours | Game-priced ransom preserves individual progression; nonpayment uses approved level-1-adult claim rule |

There is **no obligation** that loot be returned automatically. A corpse/recovery marker, world reset, PvE enemy presence and recovery window can create meaningful rescue gameplay. Exact loot persistence and insurance rules remain OPEN.

### Example of acceptable loss

A solo explorer enters a dangerous island with a modestly trained dinosaur, replaceable worker and ordinary gathered supplies. The party fails. Recoverable loot remains at the encounter, but the worker and dinosaur may be lost. Their secure home, buildings, other residents, stockpiles and functioning logging operation remain intact. A worker boat may later bring a replacement, and stored supplies can support another tame.

An elite expedition is a different decision: a renowned breeder's perfect mount, a master smith and a uniquely made weapon are assets worth withholding unless rewards justify the risk.

## Rare late-game human revival

**Owner-confirmed intent:** Killed player-controlled human survivors and human tribe members may eventually be brought back, but this is **extraordinarily expensive and should be used sparingly**. No magic/medicine/technology explanation, corpse deadline, revival availability scope, exact cost, limitations or progression-retention rule is finalized.

### Proposed player experience: The Remembrance Rite (working name)

A highly developed tribe may unlock a specialized, scarce means of recovering a **dead human individual** rather than creating a generic replacement. The ritual/advanced medical method could require a unique facility, specialist expertise, dangerous sourced materials, great expense, and a significant completion period. This is a deliberately uncertain **lore wrapper**: it may eventually be advanced medicine, spiritual resurrection, or a prehistoric fantasy technology. That choice belongs to the game's setting direction.

The rite would be meaningful because an exceptional human has a lifetime of progression, trained skills, identity, family relationships and inherited potential that ordinary recruits cannot readily replace.

### Proposed eligibility and constraints

- Revival applies to **confirmed deceased humans**, not to captives. Ransom must not be bypassed by declaring a prisoner dead.
- The revived person is the **same unique individual**. Duplicate copies, simultaneous active/dead states and repeat reward claims are impossible.
- **Proposal:** Recovering identifiable remains/death record from the expedition is required. A separate more costly remote-memory option could exist, but has not been approved.
- **Proposal:** The restored human keeps identity, pedigree, innate IVs, personal levels and learned skills, with a temporary serious recovery injury or reduced readiness. Retention of trained EVs and permanent penalties is an open choice.
- Revival requires late-game knowledge, infrastructure and **consumable high-risk materials**. It should not be an ordinary cash fee that wealthy players can ignore.
- Individual revival frequency may face a meaningful cooldown or escalating repeat cost. If recovery of remains is impossible, that loss may remain final unless the owner chooses another rule.
- Private homes remain protected from attack. Revival should not become an exploit for transporting captives or duplicating loot across islands.

### Proposed extraordinary cost curve

A **purely illustrative** component-cost index:

`Revival_resource_cost = Base_rare_material_cost × (1 + (Personal_level / 30)^2) × (1 + 0.15 × Prior_successful_revivals_of_this_person)`.

With `Base_rare_material_cost = 100`:
- Reviving a level-30 person for the first time: **200 rare-resource units**.
- First revival of a level-60 veteran: **500 units**.
- Third revival of that level-60 veteran: **650 units**.

These are **index units**, not prices, drop rates or an approved resource catalog. The same rite could also require a scarce nontradable catalyst, a purpose-built facility, a major labor/time cost, and retrieval risk. A valuable elite should be expensive enough to prompt a genuine "Is this person worth reviving?" decision.

**Economic constraint:** A rare-level revival must not become cheaper and faster than developing the next generation or replacing a typical field worker. However, recovering an irreplaceable veteran may sometimes be rational.

### Timing and corpse-status possibilities (all open)

- **Short recovery window:** Physical remains must be retrieved within a few real days; failure makes loss permanent.
- **Permanent death ledger, limited revival slots:** Death record remains but extremely scarce catalysts cap total revivals.
- **Remains + memory vault:** Protected home can preserve genetic/identity information but still requires physically recovering the right evidence and expensive ingredients.

These are mutually exclusive or combinable options for workshop discussion. **The existing 72-hour ransom rule is NOT a revival deadline.**

## A solo-friendly world with optional group power

The baseline game is **very solo-friendly**. The player, their trained dinosaurs and their individually managed tribe members can achieve a complete, satisfying non-PvP experience. The real tribe is a form of progression, not a substitute for an MMO guild.

Late-game opportunities include cooperative expeditions, player alliances, public trade, group battles, optional PvP and PvPvE remote settlements and raids. Cooperation may offer efficiency, scale and strategic possibilities, but the owner did not select mandatory group participation for ordinary gameplay.

There is no contradiction between an extremely capable single-player civilization and a world where group sieges exist. The remaining question is whether particular highest-risk multiplayer objectives require allied *players* or can be attempted (with higher difficulty) by a skilled human supported by NPCs and dinosaurs.

## Balance and workshop decisions

1. **Revival access:** Recover the corpse/remains, preserve a death record, or use both with very different costs?
2. **Revival retention:** All progression restored with long recovery; some EV/skill penalty; or level reduction?
3. **Revival rarity:** Which is the strongest constraint: rare ingredients, fixed per-person limit, cooldown, extraordinary cost, retrieval danger or all of them?
4. **Recruitment rate:** Should a basic home have occasional arrivals on a predictable timetable, or random boats when housing/food allow? How much stronger are PvPvE recruitment odds?
5. **Risk recovery:** How long does recoverable expedition loot remain available, including through unstable-island resets?
6. **Solo frontier:** May a sufficiently developed NPC-led solo tribe participate in the hardest cooperative/PvPvE objectives, or do any modes explicitly require allied human players?

> Owner decisions above are experience-level commitments. This document's numbers, roles, rituals and recovery details are designed for comparison, **not** automatic implementation.
